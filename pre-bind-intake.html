02

Findings

What we identified

From the whiteboard

What was missing in the platform

  • Line-specific needs

    Aviation and Construction customers had requirements our Marine system did not cover. Fields were built around vessels. Aircraft and building projects had nowhere to go.

  • Way of working

    New customers still worked by hand in Excel and other tools, each one for a different job.

  • Clearance step

    Aviation had a clearance team that filled in missing data before underwriters quoted. Our flow had no place for it.

  • Missing data view

    Nothing in the submission showed underwriters what was missing.

  • Key data

    Underwriters searched by hand for the data that mattered before they could quote.

  • Shared base

    The flow was built around Marine. Each new line would have needed a new design.

04

User journey

One journey, from first email to post-bind

With the client needs and the gaps in Marine clear, I mapped the full underwriter journey on the whiteboard. This sketch became the base for the flowchart.

  1. 01SubmissionSubmissions came in two ways. Brokers sent emails with several files, or underwriters created one in the platform.
  2. 02Clearance reviewFor Aviation, a clearance team checked each submission first. Missing fields were filled and key data was highlighted.
  3. 03Underwriter reviewIn the workbench, underwriters validated the data, ran checks, read the documents and decided to quote or reject.
  4. 04Quote and bindAn approved submission moved to quote, then bind, then the post-bind workflow. Post-bind existed only for Marine at the time.
Runs in the background

Guidelines were not a step in the journey. Teams set them up in a separate part of the platform. When a submission arrived, the system checked it against every guideline and highlighted any match. Clearance teams and underwriters saw those highlights at each review.

05 · My contribution

Three small fixes, now used across every line

The usability problems from the Marine review, each solved once and reused everywhere.

01

Pattern

Right side panel

Problem

Notes, attachments, guidelines and audit logs each had their own tab. Every switch felt like leaving the page, and broke the underwriter's flow.

Decision

One side panel that stays open next to the work and holds all of them. The separate tabs were removed.

Outcome

Used in every section of the platform, across all 5 lines of business. It was still the core pattern when I left.

07 · What I learned

What I would take to the next platform

  1. 01

    The biggest decision came from listening

    The clearance stage was not in any brief. It came from Aviation clients describing how their teams really worked.

  2. 02

    Differences are settings, not separate products

    Each time a line worked differently, I asked if it could be a setting on the base. Clearance became a switch, not a second product.

  3. 03

    Small fixes multiply at platform scale

    A filter that takes 8 clicks is a small annoyance on one line. Fixing it once in the base fixed it for every line.

  4. 04

    Measure from day one

    We aimed to cut checking time by half but never set up a way to track it. Next time, I would agree how to measure success before launch.

In one line

Marine had a working flow built for one line. I left a base that five lines of business and their clients run on, where adding a new line is a setup job, not a rebuild.
