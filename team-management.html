02

Findings

What we identified

What was missing in the platform

Team management

Clients had no controls of their own. Even a simple role or permission change went through our team.

User directory

Each client had many people using the platform, but no page showing who they were or what role they had. They tracked it outside the system.

Permissions

There was no place to turn a permission on or off. Every request went to our admin, then to the backend.

Custom roles

Creating a new role also came back to our admin.

Locations

Some clients work across locations and countries. They could not set up the system that way.

Teams

Some clients are organised in teams. To give a permission, they had to remember who sat in which team.

For clients

Total dependency on us

Any change their business needed meant waiting on our admins and developers.

Add 30+ membersNew rolePermission change

For our admins and developers

Extra work after every onboarding

There was no bulk upload, so a client with 30+ members meant 30+ manual set-ups. And it was never one client. We had 3 lines of business, Marine, Aviation and PVT, each with many clients.

The real challenge

Every client has its own roles, rules and way of working. How do we design one common flow that works for all of them?

04

Define

Don't build their system. Let them build their own.

How might one platform let every client shape its own team, roles and permissions?

4 tests it had to pass

1Clients change things themselves
A client's admin should add users and change roles or permissions without contacting us.
2Every client works their own way
Each client should name its own roles, set its own permissions and organise its own teams.
3No engineers in the loop
A user, role or permission change should never need backend work.
4One module for every line
The same module should work for every line of business and every client, without changes.

04 · Edge cases

Thinking past the happy path

Before designing final screens, I walked through the first months of a client admin's life on the platform. At every awkward moment, I asked what goes wrong here, and what should happen instead.

Week two

A new role is almost the same as an existing one

Without this

Every role built from scratch, one permission at a time, with small mistakes creeping in.

What I designed

Duplicate a role, rename it and remove a few permissions. Head of Underwriting becomes Senior, then Junior, in minutes.

Make the second time faster than the first.

Head of UnderwritingSenior UnderwriterDuplicateJunior UnderwriterDuplicate

Month two

Someone leaves the company

Without this

Their open submissions would sit with nobody, and a live deal could be missed.

What I designed

Their open work is never lost. If they were in a team, it goes to the team manager. If not, it goes to the admin, who reassigns it.

Nothing a person owned gets lost.

Deleted user
Team managerIn a teamAdminNo team

Month six

The company restructures and a role is removed

Without this

People would be left holding a role that no longer exists, with broken access.

What I designed

A pop-up shows everyone who holds the role. Once they are disconnected, they become Members until the admin gives them a new role.

No change happens silently.

Regional Lead3 people hold this roleMember

The happy path is the easy part. The design work is in the moments nobody plans for.

My contribution

One flow I added that no one asked for

Inline reassignment on delete

Problem

Deleting a user blocked until their work was manually reassigned elsewhere. A broken, multi-step dead end.

Decision

Brought reassignment inside the delete flow. The system surfaces active submissions and prompts inline reassignment before confirming deletion.

Outcome

One guided flow handles both tasks, reassign and delete, without leaving the page.

07 · What this taught me

Designing generic systems is the hardest UX work

  1. 01

    Generic systems need invisible flexibility

    It has to work for every client without feeling built for someone else. That takes ruthless abstraction and thoughtful defaults.

  2. 02

    Requirements don't design themselves

    The inner details weren't in any brief. They came from understanding what a manager actually needs to do their job.

  3. 03

    Critical flows must respect user intent

    The admin wants to delete, so the flow helps them do it safely instead of standing in the way.

  4. 04

    Data collected early pays dividends later

    Region and country at creation felt minor. They became essential for smart team filtering down the line.

A note from me

Looking back, I designed this system so it would not need changing, however many new lines or clients join. For me, the real test of system design is how well it handles the cases you have not built yet.
