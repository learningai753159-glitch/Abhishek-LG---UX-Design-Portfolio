What we found

Three groups. One bottleneck.

Clients

No control
  • Every new person meant a request
  • Removing someone meant asking again
  • Small permission changes needed us

Our admin team

More requests, more waiting
  • More lines and clients, each different
  • More kinds of requests, easy to miss
  • Slow hand-offs to developers and back

Our developers

Basic tasks became backend work
  • Users and permissions changed by hand
  • Extra work on top of their own
  • Big onboardings lost a lot of time

No teams

Everyone saw everything

No teams or locations. Every submission was visible to everyone, so people filtered by hand and kept track of teams in their heads.

The insight

"We have the system, but not the controls."

Every client had its own roles, rules and way of working.

The question: one common flow that works for every client.

What it cost

  • A client admin could give someone too much access, or remove a role people still needed.
  • The work of managing people moved from our team to the client admin.

How I handled it

  • I added two starter roles, Admin and Member, so day one is never blocked.
  • I added a warning before a role is removed, showing who holds it.
  • I made open work move to someone else before a user is removed.

07 · Test

Three rounds. One big fix.

  1. Round 1Our internal team

    Checked the flows and the logic.

What testing showed

The load moved to the client admin

The work our admin and developers used to do now sat with one person.

The fix · super admins

Share the work

So I added super admins. The client admin becomes a super admin and creates more admins: by location, by team, or however they work.

10 · What I learned

What this one taught me.

  1. 1Moving work isn't removing it. Self-serve first moved the load to one admin.
  2. 2Never start a client from a blank page. Two starter roles made day one easy.
  3. 3Design for people leaving as well as joining. Those moments needed the most care.
