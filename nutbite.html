01 — The Problem

Two people, one side hustle, zero visibility.

A husband and wife run a cashew manufacturing business and retail store — alongside their full-time jobs. Store staff logged sales by hand and sent WhatsApp photos of notebook pages. When photos arrived late, or not at all, a week's sales simply disappeared.

The real problem wasn't data entry. It was that the owners had no way to know what was happening in their own store.

02 — The Solution

One system. Six screens. Everything connected.

I designed and built a business management app — Product Catalogue, Inventory, Sales, Dashboard, and Reports — where every action updates the rest automatically. Record a sale, and inventory, revenue, and reports update themselves in real time.

Built responsive for both web and mobile — a deliberate call, not a default. Ask me why.

03 — Execution

Built the foundation first, not the feature.

Before any screen, I built the Product Catalogue as the single source of truth — every brand, product, pack size, and price, defined once. Inventory, Sales, and Reports all read from it. Get this layer right, and the storekeeper never types a product name or price again.

Design principle I held to: the end user had never used a business app before. If it wasn't simpler than a notebook, it had already failed. Same card-and-tap pattern across Inventory and Sales meant zero new learning between screens.

04 — The Challenge

Two very different users, one interface.

The Owners

Needed something powerful enough to change how the business runs.

The Storekeeper

Had zero computer experience and needed something she'd trust more than a pen — on day one, with no training.

Solving for one without the other would break adoption. I designed around the storekeeper's ceiling, not the owners' ambition — because if she doesn't use it, the data never reaches them.

08 — What I Learned

Real clients carry real weight.
