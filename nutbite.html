Simpler than a notebook: a store app for a first-time user

A family-run cashew business tracked every sale in notebooks and WhatsApp photos. I designed and built an app that records each sale, updates stock automatically, and gives the owners a live view of their store.

My scope: research, design and build, for web and mobile

01 · The problem

A store the owners could not see

A husband and wife run a cashew manufacturing business and a retail store, alongside their full-time jobs. Store staff wrote every sale by hand.

  • Husband and wife owners
  • Full-time jobs alongside
  • Manufacturing and retail

When photos arrived late, or not at all, a week's sales simply disappeared.

The real problem was not data entry. The owners had no way to know what was happening in their own store.

Two very different users, one app

The owners

Needed something powerful enough to change how the business runs.

The storekeeper

Had very little experience with computers. Needed something she would trust more than a pen, on day one, with no training.

Design ceiling
What the owners wantedWhat the storekeeper could use

Solving for one without the other would break adoption. I designed around the storekeeper's ceiling, not the owners' ambition. If she does not use it, the data never reaches them.

02 · Research findings

What I learned before designing anything

I spoke with the owners to understand who would use the app every day, and what the business really needed.

Designing for the person at the counter

One thing was clear early: the storekeeper would decide whether this app worked or failed.

The storekeeper

Runs the counter in a small-town store

Tech comfortLow

If it takes longer than writing in the notebook, she will go back to the notebook.

  • Computers

    Very basic knowledge of computers and the internet.

    For the app

    The counter computer must feel as simple as a phone, with big buttons and few choices on screen.

    Principle 1
  • Needs help with

    Online shopping, online transactions and most other apps. She usually asks someone to help.

    For the app

    No long forms, menus or multi-step flows. If a task needs help, it has failed.

    Principle 2
  • Comfortable with

    WhatsApp, YouTube, simple Google Pay payments and basic browsing on her phone.

    For the app

    Stick to tap-and-scroll patterns she already knows from her phone.

    Principle 3

Three principles I designed by

1

Keep it simple

One clear action per screen, big tap targets and plain words.

2

Fewest taps possible

Every flow cut to the fewest steps. Too many taps and she would lose patience.

3

Nothing new to learn

The same simple card-and-tap pattern on every screen, so learning one screen means knowing them all.

They asked for a sales record. The business needed more.

While learning how the store ran, I spotted gaps the owners had not mentioned. Each one was a chance for the app to do more than record sales, and actually help the business grow.

The brief: record sales

Running low

What I noticed

Stock ran out without warning, sometimes while a customer was waiting.

What the app does

Flags products before they run out.

For the business

Fewer lost sales at the counter.

What sells, and what does not

What I noticed

Stock was bought from habit and memory. Slow movers sat on the shelf while best sellers ran short.

What the app does

Shows the top sellers and the slow movers.

For the business

Buy more of what sells, less of what does not, and keep less money tied up in unwanted stock.

Sales reports

What I noticed

The owners only had time to review the business once a month, on a weekend.

What the app does

Weekly, monthly and yearly reports, ready any time.

For the business

A clear picture of the shop without spending a weekend on it.

A bill for every sale

What I noticed

Customers left without a proper bill.

What the app does

Every recorded sale creates a bill that can be downloaded and printed in one step. I designed the bill template, filled in automatically from the sale.

For the business

A small store that looks and runs like an organised brand.

How the owners responded

I shared the whole concept with the owners before building. They liked it enough to invest in a computer and a printer for the counter, so billing could be part of the app from day one.

03 · The goal

What the app had to do

From these findings, I set five tests the app had to pass.

1Simpler than a notebook
The storekeeper should use it on day one, with no training and the fewest taps.
2Every sale reaches the owners
Each sale should reach the owners the moment it happens, with no photos to send.
3The whole store at a glance
The owners should see sales, stock and reports any time, from anywhere.
4Know the stock
The app should show what is running low, what sells and what does not.
5A bill for every sale
Every sale should produce a printed bill, automatically.
04 · The process

From wireframes to decisions

I wireframed every screen in FigJam first, testing each one against the storekeeper's needs before building anything.

From my desk

Low-fi wireframes of each screen, made in FigJam. Click any one to see it in full.

Five decisions that shaped the app

01

The catalogue came before any screen

I built the Product Catalogue as the single source of truth: every brand, product, pack size, and price, defined once. Inventory, Sales, and Reports all read from it.

WhyGet this layer right and the storekeeper never types a product name or price again.

02

One pattern across every screen

The same card-and-tap interaction repeats on Inventory and Sales. Nothing new to learn when moving between them.

WhyThe storekeeper had never used a business app. Learning one screen had to mean knowing them all. See principle 3 in the research findings.

03

Responsive on web and mobile

Built for both from the start, a deliberate call, not a default.

WhyThe storekeeper works at the counter computer, next to the printer. The owners have full-time jobs, so they check the store on their phones, from anywhere.

04

Owner actions sit behind a PIN

Pricing, catalogue edits, and reports are gated for owners only. Everything the storekeeper needs stays one tap away.

WhyTrust on day one mattered more than access: she could not break anything that mattered.

05

The bill comes out of the sale

Recording a sale fills in the bill automatically, ready to download and print.

WhyNo extra typing for the storekeeper, and every customer leaves with a proper bill.

10 · What I learned

Real clients carry real weight

This was a real business with real money. Every decision I made had a real effect, and I had to stand behind it.

2

The brief is where you start, not where you stop

They asked for a way to record sales, and I gave them that. I also added stock alerts, top sellers, reports and printed bills, because a good tool should leave the business better than before.
